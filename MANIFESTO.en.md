# Human evolution is still underway

In the first tool revolution, people used their bodies to operate tools. In the second, they can act through tools by expressing intent.

Consider someone who has lost the ability to speak and wants to tell a family member something. She attempts to speak. An interface picks up activity associated with the attempt, and a computer converts it into words. What matters to her is that conversation becomes possible again. A researcher can pose a question and bring computation, instruments and collaborators to bear on it. Someone building a physical object can hand off surveying, moving and machining. What each person can achieve increasingly depends on the goals they can set, the systems they can call on and their ability to tell whether the work succeeded.

Humans have a long history of remote control, coordination and delegation. The change today concerns who can afford to do these things, at what scale, and how much effort it takes to get a result. A person with domain knowledge can put several tools to work on one problem without mastering every tool or assembling an organization first. The frontier extends from software to laboratories, factories, clinics and homes. ASCENSION follows what happens when this capability becomes part of a person's life.

## A life that depends on an irreplaceable body

Imagine two kinds of advanced intelligence. One depends on an irreplaceable body. Destroy that body and the individual who depended on it comes to an end. The other can continue on a replacement substrate after the original is gone. I use this distinction to think about possible futures. What kinds of replacement preserve the same individual remains an open question.

I choose the first path. A body can be repaired, altered and connected to systems that expand perception and action. Its form and composition may change. A computer can remember for me, a thermostat can manage the room, and a robot can lift things for me. An agent can decide on my behalf. An animal I live with can respond to my instructions while retaining a life of its own. All these relationships can become important to my life. The distinction concerns what my continued existence depends on.

## Trust can justify delegation

If an agent proves reliable and acts as I expect, I am willing to give it an entire class of decisions. I would consider delegating every decision to a sufficiently reliable agent. The ability to develop and sustain myself can coexist with extensive delegation.

In practice, I can rarely verify how a system was made, how its reasoning works throughout or how it will behave for years. Manufacturing defects, wear and accidents matter. Suppliers get new owners. The incentives of people operating a system can diverge from mine. A chain of seemingly credible arrangements can leave decisions that nobody involved can fully verify or constrain. Commitments can also be broken. That is the chain of doubt behind my decision to keep investing in my own capabilities.

The right arrangements depend on what is at stake. For everyday tools I may care more about replacing a supplier, recovering my records or regaining access after a failure than about approving each action. For systems that support life, I would demand longer verification, clearer authority and more ways to recover. I am willing to take reasonable risks, and to expand what I delegate as experience warrants.

## A finite god

A god in ASCENSION can live a long time and still die. Such a person could command extraordinary machines, understand complex systems, repair parts of the body and mobilize resources on scales few individuals can reach today. Each ability would still cost time, materials or energy. Both kinds of intelligence inhabit the same physical world. Both can err. Progress can be uneven, with failures and detours along the way.

Restoring someone's ability to work, making years of inquiry fit into a year and handing repetitive physical labor to machines are outcomes a person can feel. Their value comes from the life they make possible.

## The case for learning hard things now

I want to study mathematics, computing, physics, languages and engineering as AI improves. Mathematics helps me distinguish a conclusion from an approximation. Physics tells me what a proposal depends on. Engineering teaches me to assemble parts into something that works outside a demonstration. Languages give me access to more people and more evidence.

Tools will replace some tasks and lower the threshold for others. I expect people with stronger foundations to be able to set and reach a wider range of goals with the same tools. The strength of this effect depends on the task, the cost and the capabilities of the tool. We can test it against real work.

## Taking part

AGI is an important technical goal. The outcomes I care about are people's power to think, act, heal, live longer and organize resources. For a tool I want to know what its users can now do. For a business I want to know who pays, and why. For a therapy I want to know how long the benefit lasts. For the future I want to put my judgments on record in a form that can prove me wrong.

That is ASCENSION. Human evolution is still underway, and we can take part in shaping what follows.
